Basement flooding protection in Toronto

Sewer backup or a wet basement after every storm. Backwater valves, sump pumps with battery backup, and the City subsidy that pays most of it.

Signs you need this

  • Water in the basement after heavy rain
  • Sewage backed up through the floor drain
  • Sump pump runs constantly or not at all
  • Basement smells damp or musty
  • Neighbours have flooded
  • Insurer asking for flood protection

Two kinds of flooding, two fixes

Sewer backup is the City main surcharging during a storm and pushing sewage up through your lowest drain. The fix is a backwater valve on your sanitary line, which closes when flow reverses. Groundwater is the water table rising around the foundation; the fix is a sump pump with a battery backup for when the power goes out mid-storm, which is exactly when you need it.

  • Backwater valve installation (permit and City inspection included)
  • Sump pump installation, replacement and battery backup
  • Weeping-tile severance and capping
  • Drain camera inspection and sewer line repair
  • Flood-protection assessment with a written report

Questions

Do I need a permit for a backwater valve?

Yes. A plumbing permit and a City inspection are required, and the subsidy depends on them. We handle both.

Will a backwater valve stop groundwater?

No. It only stops sewer backup. If water comes through the floor or walls, you need a sump pump, and often both.

How long does the subsidy take?

The City reviews applications after the inspection; allow a few months. Our paperwork is complete so there is nothing to chase.
