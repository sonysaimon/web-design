Signs you need this

  • Water you cannot shut off
  • Burst or split pipe
  • Sewage backing up into the house
  • No water or no hot water in winter
  • Ceiling or wall suddenly wet
  • Frozen pipe that has stopped flowing

What counts as an emergency

Anything where water is going somewhere it should not, and you cannot stop it. Burst supply lines, failed water heaters, sewage backups, frozen pipes and leaks behind walls. If you are not sure, call. We will tell you honestly whether it can wait until morning.

What to do while we are on the way

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually at the front wall of the basement where the service pipe comes in, near the meter. Turn it clockwise.
  2. Cut power to any flooded area at the breaker panel, if you can reach it safely.
  3. Open a tap on the lowest floor to drain pressure from the lines.
  4. Move what you can off the wet floor and take photos for your insurer.

After-hours pricing

You are quoted a flat price before work starts, at 2 a.m. the same as at 2 p.m. Emergency calls outside regular hours carry an after-hours call-out fee that the technician states up front. Peace of Mind Plan members have it waived.

Questions

How quickly can someone get here?

Emergency calls get a same-day response, around the clock. The dispatcher gives you a real arrival window when you call, not a vague promise.

Do you charge more at night?

The repair price is the same. There is an after-hours call-out fee, stated before we dispatch, so you can decide.

Can you help with insurance?

Yes. You get a written description of the cause and the repair, plus photos, which is what adjusters ask for.
