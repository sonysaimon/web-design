# John The Plumber Toronto — pitch script

Demo: http://localhost:8081/ (deploy with ./deploy.sh → web.soichirosaimon.com/john-the-plumber-toronto/ before the call so they can open it on their phone)
Current page: https://johntheplumber.ca/toronto-plumber/
Audit: REPORT.md (50/100 capped)

## Who you are talking to
John The Plumber is an Ottawa company with branches. The Toronto branch may be a franchisee or manager who does not control johntheplumber.ca. Find that out in the first two minutes. If they do not control it, the pitch becomes "a Toronto branch page you can show head office" or "your own Toronto site that links back".

## 1. Opener (phone or walk-in, 20 seconds)
"Hi, is this the person who looks after John The Plumber's Toronto branch? My name is Sony, I'm a web designer here in Toronto. I'm not selling anything on this call. I built a working version of what your Toronto website could look like, using your own reviews and job photos, and I'd like to show it to you for five minutes. Do you have a phone in front of you?"

If "not interested / we have a site":
"Totally fair, you do have one. Can I just tell you the one thing I found on it? It takes ten seconds." → go to the hook.

## 2. The hook (the one thing)
"Right now your Toronto branch shows four different phone numbers depending on where a customer looks: the website header, Google, the page code, and your van. And two different addresses. A customer who googles you at 11 pm with water on the floor has to guess which one is right. That's the first thing I fixed."

Pause. Let them react. They will either confirm which number is right (great, write it down) or be surprised (great, you just proved you did your homework).

## 3. Walk the demo (3 minutes, they are on their phone)
Send them the link, then narrate top to bottom:
- "First screen: your real crew photo, your actual 4.8 from 266 reviews, one phone number, one Book button."
- "Tap a problem, 'No hot water' or 'Basement flooding'. It shows one of your real before/after jobs with the neighbourhood and what happens first. Then Book already knows what the issue is."
- "Scroll down: your reviews, with your technicians' names pulled out. Marcus, Liam, Michael, Adam, Jeff. People book people, not companies."
- "Service area page: a map and six Toronto districts with local copy. Your current page lists the areas in one sentence."
- "Sump pump page: the City of Toronto flooding subsidy, up to $6,650, laid out in a table. None of your competitors do that properly."
- "Everything loads in under a second. Your current page is half a megabyte of HTML with 95 scripts."

## 4. Why it matters (money, not design)
"You already pay for the leads: Google, reviews, the vans. The website is where a chunk of them leak out. Fixing the number confusion and giving people one obvious Book button is the cheapest lead you'll ever buy."

## 5. The ask
"I'd like to finish this for you. I need about six answers from you, the right phone number, the address, your licence number, and how you want bookings to arrive, and it can be live within a week. Can we do fifteen minutes this week to go through those?"

Then stop talking.

## 6. Price (fill in before the call)
- Build: $____ one-time (demo already 80% done, so you can be decisive on price)
- Hosting + edits: $____/month, or none if they host it themselves
- Anchor: "A single emergency call-out is worth more than a month of this."

## 7. Objections
- "Head office handles the website." → "Perfect, then this is easy for you. Forward them the link and the audit. Or, if you'd rather own your Toronto presence, this can live on its own domain and link back to them. Which would you prefer?"
- "We already rank fine." → "You do rank. The problem is what happens after the click. Four numbers, a Book button that goes nowhere without JavaScript, and four Toronto pages competing against each other for the same search."
- "How much?" (early) → "Depends on one thing: whether this is a branch page or your own site. Let me show you first, then I'll give you a number on the spot."
- "Send me something." → "I'll send the link and a one-page audit right now. Can I call you Thursday at 10 to hear what you thought?"
- "We use Jobber / our booking modal." → "Good, it plugs in. The Book button can open your Jobber request or your existing modal. The demo uses a form only because your booking link isn't public."
- "Why is the phone number different on your demo?" → "I used the Google one because that's what customers see. You tell me which is right and it changes in one place."

## 8. Do not say
- Do not call their site "bad" or "ugly". Say "it's leaking leads" or "it's working against itself".
- Do not say you scraped or audited them. Say "I went through your Toronto page the way a customer would".
- Do not promise rankings or a number of leads.
- Do not quote the City subsidy amounts as fact. Say "I pulled the current amounts, we'll verify them against toronto.ca before launch."
- Do not pitch a rebuild of johntheplumber.ca. The parent site is good. This is a Toronto branch page.
