How fob copying works

A fob holds a small chip and coil. When it’s near the reader, the chip powers up and sends a unique ID, and the building checks that ID against its list. No personal information is stored on the fob. We read that ID with specialised equipment and write it to a new fob, then test it three times.

How long does it take to copy my fob?

Usually no more than 2–5 minutes for 95% of fobs, including triple verification. A few high-frequency fobs take a little longer because of their encryption; we’ll tell you when you book.

What kind of fobs can you copy?

About 99% of the key fobs used in buildings today, including HID Prox, Indala, ioProx, Paradox, AWID, ICT and Pyramid. If yours isn’t listed, text or email us a photo.

What if my new fob doesn’t work?

Every copy is tested three times before you get it, and all work is backed by a 100% money-back guarantee. If the copy doesn’t work completely, you get your money back, no questions asked.

Will the building know it’s a copy?

Other than how it looks, the copy is identical. The access system reads the same ID as your original.

If my original fob is deactivated, will the copy still work?

No. A copy carries the exact data of the original, so if the building deactivates the original, both stop working.

Can you put two fobs on one?

No, RFID access control doesn’t allow two fobs on one chip. If your key chain is heavy, ask about our ultra-light fobs and stickers.

Do you do car remotes and keys?

Yes. Text us your vehicle’s year, make and model and whether it’s push-to-start.
