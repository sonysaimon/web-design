Counts as an emergency

  • Burst or frozen pipe, no water at all
  • Sewage coming up a floor drain or toilet
  • Water heater leaking or spraying
  • Sump pump failed during rain
  • Main water supply leak
  • Flooded basement from plumbing

While you wait

Burst pipe: close the main shut-off (usually where the water line enters the basement, near the meter), open a low tap to drain pressure, and move what you can away from the water.

Sewage backup: stop running water anywhere in the house, keep people and pets out of the area, open a window. Do not try to plunge a main drain backup.

Water heater leak: turn off the cold supply valve on top of the tank and the gas or breaker. Leave the tank alone otherwise.

Sump pump down in a storm: check the breaker and the float. If it is dead, call; we carry pumps on the truck.

What happens next

The technician who is coming calls you first, asks what you are seeing, and gives you an arrival window. On site, the first job is to stop the water. Then you get a written price for the repair before any more work happens. If a full repair has to wait for parts or daylight, we make the house safe and come back, usually the next day.

Is it an emergency?

A running toilet, a dripping tap or a slow drain are not emergencies, and you will save money booking them as a regular same-day visit. Water where it should not be, or no water at all, is. When in doubt, call and ask; the dispatcher will tell you honestly.
