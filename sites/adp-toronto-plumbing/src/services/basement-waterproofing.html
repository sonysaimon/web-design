Basement waterproofing.

A damp line along the bottom of the wall every March, a crack that weeps after a storm, a basement you can't finish because you don't trust it. We stop the water on the outside where we can, manage it on the inside where we can't, and pair it with a sump system the City helps pay for.

Where the water is coming from

Toronto basements get wet for three reasons, and the fix depends on which one you have. Groundwater coming through the foundation wall or the floor-wall joint, especially in the spring thaw, means the weeping tile around the footing has silted up or was never there. A crack in a poured wall means shrinkage or settlement. And water that shows up only in storms, from the floor drain, isn't a waterproofing problem at all: it's the City sewer surcharging, which is a backwater valve job.

We look before we quote. Sometimes that's a camera down the weeping tile; sometimes it's a conversation about which wall gets wet and when.

How we fix it

  • Exterior waterproofing. Excavate to the footing, clean and repair the wall, apply a waterproof membrane and drainage board, lay new weeping tile in clear gravel, backfill and restore. The permanent fix.
  • Interior drainage system. Cut the slab along the wall, install a drainage channel to a new sump pit, seal the wall-floor joint. For walls you can't dig: party walls, under a deck or driveway.
  • Crack injection. Polyurethane injected into a leaking crack from inside, usually done in a few hours.
  • Window wells and grading. New wells with drains tied to the weeping tile; the cheapest fixes are often above ground.
  • Sump pump and backup. Installed with the system and filed for the Toronto subsidy, up to $2,550 for pump and battery.

Questions

Interior or exterior waterproofing?
Exterior stops water before it reaches the wall and is the permanent fix, but needs access to dig. Interior collects water that gets through and sends it to a sump; it's the answer where you can't excavate, such as a shared wall or a finished driveway.
Does the Toronto subsidy cover waterproofing?
Not the membrane itself. But the sump pump ($2,250), battery backup ($300) and weeping-tile severance ($400) that usually go with it are covered, and we file for them.
How long does exterior waterproofing take?
Two to five days for a typical wall, depending on depth, access and weather. The crew protects the neighbour's side yard and puts the ground back.
Is a crack in the foundation serious?
A hairline shrinkage crack that leaks can usually be injected from inside in a few hours. A crack that is wide, growing or horizontal needs a look at the foundation itself; we'll tell you which it is.
