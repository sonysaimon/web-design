Why the service pipe is the problem

The water service is the single pipe that brings water from the street into your house. In older Toronto neighbourhoods it is often the original ½-inch lead or galvanized pipe, laid when the house was built. Lead is a health issue; galvanized rusts shut from the inside until the pressure upstairs is a trickle. Either way, no amount of fixture work fixes it, because the restriction is underground.

Upgrading to a ¾-inch or 1-inch copper service restores pressure for the whole house, removes the lead, and is a requirement for many basement renovations and additions.

How we do it

  • Locate and quote. We find the City shutoff and the entry point, confirm the pipe material and give you a flat-rate price. Free.
  • Permit. We pull the plumbing permit and notify the City of the service work.
  • Trenchless where possible. Most services are pulled with a new copper line from a pit at the property line to a pit at the foundation, without a trench across the lawn. Directional drilling for longer runs.
  • Connect, even under pressure. If the City shutoff fails, which it often does on old services, the crew connects live rather than waiting days for the City.
  • Inspection, backfill, restore. City inspects, we backfill, compact and put the lawn and walkway back.

Questions

How do I know if my water service is lead?
Find where the pipe enters the basement, before the meter. Lead is dull grey, soft enough to scratch shiny with a key, and doesn't ring when tapped. Toronto homes built before the mid-1950s often have one. We'll confirm it during the free estimate.
Does the City replace its side?
The City owns the pipe from the main to the property line; you own the pipe from the property line into the house. We replace your side, and can coordinate with the City on theirs.
Will I be without water?
For part of one day in most cases. We connect the new copper and restore water before the crew leaves.
What if the City shutoff doesn't work?
It happens often on old services. Our crews connect under pressure when they have to, so the job doesn't stall waiting for the City.
