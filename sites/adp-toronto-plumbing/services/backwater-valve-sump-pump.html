Backwater valve and sump pump installation.

When the City sewer surcharges in a storm, the only thing between it and your basement is a backwater valve. Toronto pays 80% of the cost to put one in, up to $6,650 per property across valve, sump pump and capping. We install, pull the permit, and file the application.

The subsidy, item by item

The City of Toronto's Basement Flooding Protection Subsidy Program was expanded on May 1, 2026. It now pays 80% of the invoiced cost, including labour, materials, permit and taxes, up to these maximums:

  • Backwater valve, $1,600 per device, maximum two. Installation or replacement on the sanitary line.
  • Sump pump, $2,250. Installation or replacement of a sump pump, pit and discharge.
  • Battery backup for the sump pump, $300. So it runs through the power cut that comes with the storm.
  • Weeping tile severance and capping, $400. Disconnecting your foundation drain from the City sewer so it can't be used as a way in.
  • Home plumbing assessment, $500. A one-time visit where a licensed plumber tells you which of the above your house actually needs.

Maximum $6,650 per property, for single-family, duplex and triplex homes inside the City of Toronto. Work has to be done by a licensed plumber with a permit. The calculator on our home page adds it up for your house; the City's own terms are on toronto.ca.

Outside Toronto: Vaughan has run its own backwater-valve subsidy. Ask us for the current terms when you call.

What we do, in order

  • Assessment and camera inspection. We locate the sanitary line, check the weeping tile connection and tell you what the house needs. The assessment itself is subsidy-eligible.
  • Permit. We pull the plumbing permit; the City won't pay without one.
  • Install. For a mainline valve we cut the basement floor at the sanitary line, set the valve with a cleanout beside it and patch the concrete. For a sump system we cut the pit, set the liner, pump and check valve, and run the discharge outside.
  • City inspection. We book it and meet the inspector.
  • Subsidy application. We fill in the forms, attach the invoice and permit, and submit. Our reviews mention this one more than anything else.

Questions

Who qualifies for the Toronto subsidy?
Owners of single-family, duplex and triplex homes in the City of Toronto, for eligible work done by a licensed plumber with a permit. The subsidy is 80% of the invoiced cost up to the per-item maximums, to $6,650 per property.
How long does a backwater valve take to install?
Usually one day. The basement floor is cut open at the sanitary line, the valve and a cleanout are set, the concrete is patched, and the City inspects before the floor is closed.
Do I need both a valve and a sump pump?
A backwater valve stops the City sewer coming back up your drain. A sump pump deals with groundwater coming through the foundation. Many Toronto houses need both; the home plumbing assessment tells you which.
What does the subsidy pay for a battery backup?
Up to $300, on top of the $2,250 for the sump pump. The backup keeps the pump running during the power outages that come with the storms that flood basements.
